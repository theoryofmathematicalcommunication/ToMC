Notes on Dark City

Axel Tamir · 1 min read

Written after a second viewing, mostly as a way of working out why the film's ending bothers me.

The Strangers' mistake

The Strangers experiment on Murdoch because they believe the self is located somewhere — that if they inject enough memories, they will find the residue that makes a person that person. Their whole research programme presupposes that identity is a substance.

What they keep discovering, and keep failing to interpret, is that it isn't. Murdoch is not the sum of his memories, and the film's cruellest joke is that the Strangers are the only characters who take memory entirely seriously.

The question is never who was I before the injection, but what am I in the act of asking.

Why the ending bothers me

Having spent ninety minutes dismantling the idea that a self is a retrievable object, the film hands Murdoch the power to remake the city by will alone — a self so substantial it becomes a physical force.

It's a beautiful sequence. It's also, I think, a retreat: the anxiety of the first two acts gets resolved by simply granting the wish the Strangers could not fulfil.

Something to come back to alongside the McGowan essay, which reads the same ending more generously than I can.
